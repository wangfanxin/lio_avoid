FLIGHT D -- REFUTED: OPENING THE ACCELERATION MASK MAKES THE TRACKING WORSE AND THE AIRCRAFT HITS A PILLAR
(2026-10-16, pre-registered in `docs/metrics/flightD-prereg-20261016.txt` before arming)

Run `logs/missionA-FLIGHTD-posvelacc`, rc=0, no abort, landed.  ONE variable against Flight C:
`BRIDGE_FIELDS=posvelacc`, i.e. the MAVLink `type_mask` 2496 -> **2048** (`POS_VEL_ACC_YAW_MASK =
IGNORE_YAW_RATE`, `mavlink_impl.py:57`), so the acceleration column that has always been in the message is finally
used by the flight controller.  Everything else identical: the cleaned tree, H1, H2, the 0.35 m reference clamp,
the raised flight-controller limits, the plan's own heading, `EGO_MAX_VEL=0.8` / `EGO_MAX_ACC=1.0`.

THE MEASUREMENT (`_stage/flightD-vs-C.py`)

    run                        truth min   in body   <0.10 m   wire mask   |a| p99   |a| max   tracking p95
    C  posvel   (a ignored)      +0.168 m    0.00 %    0.00 %        2496      1.02      1.15       0.132 m
    D  posvelacc (a USED)        -0.226 m    0.47 %    1.40 %        2048      2.02      3.79       0.350 m

  * the arm was the arm: the wire's mask is 2048 on every row, and the acceleration it carried is non-zero
    (p99 2.02, max 3.79 m/s^2) against near-hovering values in C;
  * **the tracking got 2.7x worse** (p95 0.132 -> 0.350 m) and the aircraft entered a pillar (0.47 % of samples
    inside a body, -0.226 m);
  * so the three things that were supposed to make `posvelacc` safe this time -- the reference clamp, the small
    `max_acc`, the raised flight-controller limits -- do NOT make it safe, and the reason is measurable:

THE MECHANISM, AND IT IS THE CLAMP'S OWN BLIND SPOT.  The clamp limits the forwarded **position** only
(`ego_contract_shim.py::rewrite`), while the **acceleration field rides unclamped in the same message**.  The age
rule produces its spike at every replan boundary, and with the mask open the flight controller acts on it:
ArduPilot's `shape_pos_vel_accel_xy` adds the acceleration we send to the velocity target with
`limit_total=false` (i.e. **unshaped**), so a 3.79 m/s^2 spike at a boundary goes straight into the controller --
whereas with the mask closed the FCU's own acceleration-limited reference model had been *reconstructing* the
acceleration from position+velocity and shaping it through `WP_ACC`/`PSCN_NE_JERK`.

THIS IS THE SECOND INDEPENDENT REFUTATION OF `posvelacc`, and the cleaner one: DIAG33 aborted on the geofence with
a 22 m/s^2 alternating reference, and the standing explanation was that the age rule's jumps were the cause.  That
explanation is now confirmed from the other side -- with the jumps clamped to 0.36 m the failure did not go away,
it merely became a penetration instead of an abort, because the spike lives in the acceleration channel and not in
the position channel.

WHAT FOLLOWS (and it is a small change, not a new direction): the same clamp belongs on the acceleration and the
velocity that are forwarded, not only on the position -- a rate/limit filter on all three fields.  Until that
exists, `BRIDGE_FIELDS=posvel` is the arm to fly, and **Flight C remains the best configuration measured**:
min +0.168 m, 0 samples inside a body, C2-ego MET.

=== CORRECTION (2026-10-16, appended): THE CAUSE OF FLIGHT D WAS NOT THE ACCELERATION MASK ==================

An independent audit (`_stage/AUDIT-flightD-vs-C-20261016.md`, instruments `_stage/auditA..auditZZ4-*.py`) tested the
mechanism this file asserted and found it HALF RIGHT about the path and WRONG about the trigger.  What it
established, in its own numbers:

  * THE UNSHAPED PATH IS REAL, verified in the firmware: `mode_guided.cpp:943` -> `AC_PosControl.cpp:633-641` ->
    `AP_Math/control.cpp:346-411` with `limit_output=false`; our acceleration is added at `:400`, i.e. AFTER the
    corner-acceleration limit at `:399`, and with the total cap off, so only the jerk limit acts on it.  The arm's
    identity check passes: `GUIP` aX/aY/aZ is exactly 0.0 in Flight C and max 3.422 in Flight D.
  * **IT IS NOT A REPLAN-BOUNDARY SPIKE.**  |a(after) - a(before)| across commits: C median 0.013, p90 0.540, max
    1.036 m/s2; D median 0.015, p90 0.671, max 1.791 -- the same shape, and C's own time-since-commit profile
    PEAKS MID-SPLINE (median 0.278 in [0.70,1.00) s against 0.129 in [0.05,0.10) s).  The claim in this file that
    "the spike lives in the acceleration channel at every boundary" is therefore WITHDRAWN.
  * WHY |a| EXCEEDS `max_acc` AT ALL, in BOTH runs and unrelated to the mask: `uniform_bspline.cpp:166-190` checks
    the acceleration PER AXIS (never the norm, so two axes at their 1.05 tolerance give 1.15), and
    `planner_manager.cpp:651`'s `refineTrajAlgo` re-optimises AFTER that check at `:423` and never re-checks --
    which is where D's 3.795 m/s2 in a 0.598 s spline (traj_id 31) comes from.  A defect of upstream's, recorded
    here because it is new.
  * **THE DOMINANT DIFFERENCE IS OFF THE WIRE COMPLETELY: FLIGHT D'S LIO ODOMETRY DIVERGED FROM THE TRUTH BY 3.65 m**
    (onset sim 48.2 s, within 1 s of the second ground impact), and the EKF, the commanded point and the aircraft
    all followed the bad estimate.  Flight C's LIO stayed within 6 mm all flight.  In D's bad window (sim 49-50 s)
    the aircraft was ON THE GROUND -- truth altitude 0.000 m, truth speed 0.000 m/s -- while its own EKF ran at
    1.5-2.7 m/s, so the controller dragged the real aircraft east at ~2 m/s to catch up with a hallucinated state,
    back through the pillar it was resting against.  **That is a ground impact plus an estimator fault, not an
    acceleration-feed-forward failure.**
  * THE CONTROLLER PARAMETERS ARE IDENTICAL: 1328 shared parameter names in the two runs' BINs, only 10 differ and
    all are noise or counters (baro ground pressure, IMU caltemp/offsets <= 2.8e-4, MOT_THST_HOVER, STAT_*).
    `WP_ACC`, `PSC_NE_JERK`, `ATC_ANGLE_MAX`, `EK3_SRC1_*` identical.
  * AND THE MASK IS NOT PROVEN HARMFUL: **Flight D's own LEG 2, with the mask still open and a clean LIO, is the
    best flying of either run (+0.313 m minimum clearance, tracking max 0.146 m).  Every bad number in D is inside
    leg 1**, after the two mid-flight ground impacts; D's plan itself was also dirty in that window (min -0.295 m,
    27.5 % of plan points inside the 0.20 m footprint, against C's +0.159 m / 1.73 %), it committed at 0.86 Hz
    against C's 1.11, and its A* errors went 10 -> 950.  C, for its part, also feeds 0.78 m/s and 1.15 m/s2 forward
    while tracking to 0.033 m median, so forwarding acceleration is not by itself a tracking problem.

SO THE DISPOSITION CHANGES: the acceleration mask is UNTESTED rather than refuted -- its one flight was ruined by an
estimator divergence and two ground impacts, and its own clean-LIO leg was the best of the run.  What Flight D
actually exposes is a gap this project has never documented: **there is no estimator-health monitor anywhere
between a 3.65 m LIO fault and a pillar** -- the supervisor gates on the truth's geofence, altitude and the plan's
silence, and the FCU gates on its own EKF, but nothing compares the LIO against anything.  (On a real vehicle the
truth is unavailable, so the deployable form of that monitor is the LIO's own status/residuals and the EKF's
innovations, not a truth residual; in simulation the truth makes it measurable, which is how it was caught here.)

NEXT MEASUREMENT, WITH ITS PREDICTION RECORDED BEFORE THE FLIGHT (the audit's own proposal): re-fly Flight C's
configuration unchanged (`BRIDGE_FIELDS=posvel`) and measure |LIO(as W) - truth| per second with
`_stage/auditZZ4-onset.py`.  Prediction: the closed arm reproduces < 0.05 m for the whole run (the archived Flight C
did: median 0.0038 m, max 0.0255 m).  Refutation: any 1 s bucket above 0.5 m -- which would say C's +0.168 m was
luck, and that the real gap is the missing health monitor rather than any parameter of the planner or the mask.

=== ADDENDUM (same audit, appended because it corrects a sentence above) ==================================

Flight D's LEG 2 -- which the paragraphs above call "the best flying of either run" -- must NOT be read as the
stack recovering after the pillar.  In leg 2 the vehicle tracks the wire to a median 0.036 m **while the wire, the
FCU's EKF and the aircraft are all 3.6 m away from where the aircraft actually is**.  That is excellent tracking of
a reference in the WRONG PLACE, and it is exactly what a large unmonitored estimator fault looks like from inside
the stack: every loop closes, and every loop closes on the wrong state.  Leg 2 cleared the pillars only because the
post-divergence offset happened to point in a direction that missed them -- not because anything recovered.

So the two readings that must be struck from this file are: "the mask is not proven harmful because leg 2 flew well"
and any implication that D's later numbers are evidence about the acceleration feed-forward.  D's leg 2 is evidence
about the ESTIMATOR, and its lesson is the opposite of reassuring: a 3.65 m fault produced a run in which the
tracking error, the arrival logic and the mission's own completion all looked healthy.

The disposition that survives, therefore, is:
  * THE MASK IS **UNTESTED** -- its one flight was destroyed by an estimator divergence and two ground impacts, and
    nothing in that flight separates the mask's effect from the fault's;
  * FLIGHT C REMAINS THE BEST MEASURED CONFIGURATION (+0.168 m, 0 samples inside a body, C2-ego MET);
  * AND THE FINDING THAT OUTRANKS BOTH: **this stack has no estimator-health monitor.**  The supervisor gates on the
    truth's geofence, the altitude and the plan's silence; the FCU gates on its own EKF; the planner trusts the
    odometry it is given.  Between a 3.65 m LIO fault and a pillar there is no check at all -- and the fault was
    visible for ~2 s before the impact (onset sim 48.2 s, second ground impact within 1 s) while every consumer
    happily followed it.
  * The deployable form of such a monitor is NOT a truth residual (a real vehicle has no truth): it is the LIO's own
    status/residuals (`/lio/status` exists in this stack) and the EKF's innovations (`XKF1`/`XKFS` are logged).  In
    simulation the truth makes the fault measurable, which is how it was caught here; the monitor itself must be
    built from quantities a real vehicle has.
