THE VERTICAL CLIMB LEG'S TIMEOUT: THE CLOCK ARMED ON THE PAD
==============================================================================
Written 2026-10-15 by the round that fixed it.  Every number is read from `RECT33-accept`'s own files by
`_stage/why-no-climb.py` and `_stage/dump-logs.sh`; the run directory is
`/home/fanxin/lio_avoid/logs/missionA-RECT33-accept`.  NO FLIGHT HAS BEEN MADE WITH THE FIX YET, so this
file settles the MECHANISM and the code change, and it explicitly does NOT claim the profile now works.


1. THE SYMPTOM, IN THE RUN'S OWN WORDS
--------------------------------------
`traj.log:15-17` (the plan box's log):

  LEG 1 (waypoint 0 -> 1 ): the vehicle is 0.000 m from the leg's start -- flight clock ARMED ...
  LEG 1 arrival rule: TESTING the HEIGHT (this leg has no horizontal extent); horizontal error 0.0033 m,
      3-D error 2.3872 m, height error 2.3872 m, gate 0.260 m;
      leg_start_w=[-0.0026 -0.002 0.1128] target_w=[0.  0.  2.5] p_w=[-0.0026 -0.002   0.1128]
  LEG 1 TIMEOUT: THE VEHICLE NEVER ARRIVED -- it is 1.817 m from the commanded waypoint 1 after 20.0 s of
      flight (gate 0.060 m), 4003 pose samples on /lio/robo/odom, the newest 0.0 s old.

The arrival RULE was right (the height, gate 0.260 m = the 0.060 m gate + the 0.20 m vertical slack) and the
TARGET was right (`wire.csv` row 1 carries `p_w=(0.000000, 0.000000, 2.500000)`).  The leg still failed.


2. WHAT THE VEHICLE WAS DOING: 0.885 s OF CLIMB, NOT 20 s
---------------------------------------------------------
The bridge relayed exactly 2169 class-84 setpoints (`bridge.log:7`, `sent=2169`), and the FIRST one on the
wire is at sim t 30.301 s (`setpoints.csv`/`wire.csv` row 1, `t_sim_us=30301208`):

  t_sim 30.301 s   cmd=(0.000, 0.000, 2.500)   fcu_z=0.279   truth_z=0.192   <- the vertical leg
  t_sim 31.186 s   cmd=(0.500, 0.400, 2.000)   fcu_z=0.718   truth_z=0.769   <- waypoint 2, and the climb
                                                                                only now begins in earnest

So the climb leg was ON THE WIRE for 0.885 s.  The timeout's own residual closes the arithmetic:
2.500 - 1.817 = 0.683 m, and the leg-2 line reports the same height from the other side,
`p_w=[-0.005 0.0035 0.6839]`.  THE VEHICLE CLIMBED 0.68 m OF THE COMMANDED 2.50 m AND THE LEG GAVE UP.
It then climbed normally to the corner altitude once waypoint 2's setpoint was in force (B3's vertical
column: -0.104 ... -0.208 m, and every corner leg passed), which is why A4 passed and the aircraft hovered:
the mission was flown by corner 1's setpoint, NOT by the climb leg.


3. WHY THE CLOCK HAD ALREADY RUN: `arm_near_m` ASKED OF A LEG WHOSE TARGET IS THE PAD
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`planner/cmd_stream.py::TourPacer._arm` arms a leg's flight clock on ANY of three facts, one of which is
"it is already within `arm_near_m` of the waypoint" (`arm_near_m` = 0.30 m, from
`rectangle_source.py:353 arm_near_m=args.leg_start_m`, default 0.30).  For the take-off's climb leg the
target IS the pad -- `target_w = (0, 0, 2.5)` and the parked vehicle is 0.0033 m from it in the horizontal
plane (`leg_start_w` above) -- so that rule is satisfied WHILE THE AIRCRAFT IS PARKED.  The clock armed at
the pad, and the leg's own log records the pad as its start, not flight: `leg_start_w` z = 0.1128 m, which is
the calibration's own offset (t_WL z = +0.1156 m) plus the parked LIO z, i.e. the ground.

TIME ARITHMETIC.  The timeout fires exactly 20.0 s after arming (same log, `after 20.0 s of flight`,
`4003` pose samples at ~200 Hz), and it fired at sim t 31.186 s, so the clock armed at sim t ~11.2 s:
19.1 s before the first setpoint could reach the FCU (the relay only opens when the aircraft is airborne --
`supervisor.log:27-30`, and `bridge.log:5` `relay: ENABLED (the supervisor's /plan/trigger)`), and ~6.5 s
before the supervisor's own airborne line (`[17.72] airborne: truth altitude 0.205 m`, sim ~30.3 s).  The
vertical leg was therefore given, at most, 0.885 s to accomplish a 2.30 m climb.  No gate or timeout
tuning can fix that; the clock must not start until the vehicle has left the pad, which is exactly what the
rule's own docstring says it is for.

NOT THE OTHER SUSPECTS, each measured (not argued):
  * the target on the wire: right, 2.500 m, `wire.csv` row 1;
  * the arrival test: right, the height with a 0.26 m gate, and the same test then arrived on legs 2..6;
  * the relay: it opened at the airborne trigger and carried 2169 packets, no `refused`;
  * the bridge's L->W conversion: `p_w_z=2.500000` on the wire, and every corner was reached to 0.6-3.8 mm.


4. THE FIX (planner/cmd_stream.py)
----------------------------------
ONE RULE, IN ONE PLACE: `TourPacer.vertical_leg()` answers "does the leg in hand have no horizontal
extent?" -- the same question, with the same `arrive_m / 10` tolerance, that the arrival block already asks
to decide whether the arrival is measured on the HEIGHT or on the horizontal distance.  `_arm` now asks it
too:

    if not self.vertical_leg() and norm(p_w[:2] - target_w[:2]) <= arm_near_m:  return True

so a climb leg arms on the two rules that are facts about having LEFT THE PAD -- `arm_move_m` (0.50 m of
motion since the plan box first saw the vehicle) or `arm_above_m` (0.50 m of absolute W altitude, which
covers a plan box that starts while the aircraft is already flying).  Every leg with horizontal extent is
unchanged: the arrival gate, the settle gate and the 20 s timeout are untouched, and `vertical_slack` still
applies to the climb leg only.
