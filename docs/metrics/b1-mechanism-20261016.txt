================================================================================
== 0. THE ROW, AND THE FOUR NUMBERS IT IS MADE OF
================================================================================
tools/metrics.py prints one number per axis, and that number factorises EXACTLY:

    growth_E = max(0, x0 - min E) + max(0, max E - x1)        (the two E walls)
    growth_N = max(0, y0 - min N) + max(0, max N - y1)        (the two N walls)

so B1's 110 mm of E over RECT26 is TWO numbers, and this file is the story of those two numbers.  The
tool checks the identity on every run; the residual is printed and is 0.000 mm everywhere (the extremes
are inside the window, so the identity is exact by construction, not by luck).

== 2. THE MECHANISM, NAMED: A STEP COMMAND AND AN UNDER-DAMPED ARRIVAL RING
================================================================================
Two measured facts, then the mechanism.

(a) THE COMMAND IS A STEP.  Every setpoint in every one of these runs carries position only:
      RECT26 setpoints.csv, all 1925 rows: max |commanded velocity| = 0.0000 m/s, max |commanded
      acceleration| = 0.0000 m/s^2; and on the wire the recorded `fields` column is 'pos' for all 1925
      rows (wire.csv) with v_l = 0.0000 m/s.  The same is true of RECT23 (2048 rows) and RECT7 (2994).
    So each leg is a 1.2-2.0 m position step presented to the FCU's PSC, with no velocity or acceleration
    feed-forward: the aircraft accelerates to 1.3-1.7 m/s, brakes into the corner and then has nothing
    but its own position loop to settle on.  This is the plan box's design (planner/cmd_stream.py: "the
    dwell is CONTENT: position = the waypoint, velocity = 0"), and it is measured here from the runs'
    own recordings rather than quoted from the design.

(b) THE BRAKING ENTRY SPEED DID NOT CHANGE, so the arrival ring did not either.  The peak speed within
    1 m of each corner (the same quantity tools/metrics.py prints in its B1 REPORTED line) is, per run:
      RECT26: c2 1.73, c3 1.29, c4 1.69, closing c1 1.26 m/s
      RECT23: c2 1.75, c3 1.28, c4 1.70, closing c1 1.28 m/s
      RECT24: c2 1.74, c3 1.28, ...
    while the speed AT the closest approach (what the F2 settle gate actually sees) is 0.083-0.151 m/s on
    RECT26.  That is the reconciliation of the two refuted hypotheses: the F2 dwell rule ("the clock
    starts on the SETTLED arrival") cut the speed the GATE crosses at by ~10x, but it did not touch the
    braking from 1.3-1.7 m/s that excites the loop, which is why B1 moved only 2.154 -> 2.110 m.

(c) THE RING ITSELF, measured in the frame of the leg that arrives (the tool's TABLE E: the truth's
    position relative to the commanded corner, projected on the direction of travel 'along' and across
    it, in a window from 2 s before to 3 s after the corner pass):

      RECT26  corner  pass        short (inside)        past (outside)      delay   half-period  peak-to-peak
      c2              2.9 mm      -101.2 mm @37.15 v0.016  +35.1 mm @39.40 v0.012  0.83 s   2.25 s      136 mm
      c3              5.3 mm      -133.7 mm @47.10 v0.008  +45.9 mm @49.26 v0.007  0.80 s   2.16 s      180 mm
      c4              0.0 mm      -141.5 mm @54.20 v0.007  +75.3 mm @56.32 v0.004  0.91 s   2.11 s      217 mm
      closing c1      0.1 mm       -99.4 mm @65.15 v0.002  +42.2 mm @67.44 v0.002  0.91 s   2.29 s      142 mm

    THE MECHANISM: the aircraft flies THROUGH the commanded corner (0-5 mm from it), swings back to
    100-142 mm INSIDE the rectangle, comes forward again and overshoots 35-75 mm PAST the corner, and only
    then settles -- with the truth speed essentially ZERO at both extremes (2-16 mm/s).  The two extremes
    are 2.1-2.3 s apart, i.e. the half-period of a ~4.3 s oscillation: this is an under-damped position
    loop ring, and the outer half of it, at each of the four corners, is exactly what B1's box is made of.
    Consistency check on the shape: an overshoot that peaks a quarter-period after the pass should peak
    0.80-0.91 s after it, and it does (the 'delay' column) -- the excursion is not an offset that the
    dwell holds, it is the outer turning point of a ring that decays through the dwell (the parent unit's
    independent measurement of RECT34 c2 -- peak +92.8 mm, settled +6.7 mm -- is that same decay).

    The pre-F1 sorties show the same ring with a larger envelope (RECT7 c4: short -252.1 mm, past +83.3 mm,
    delay 0.91 s, half-period 2.33 s, p-p 335 mm; RECT7 closing c1: -81.6 / +43.4 mm), and so do F1's
    RECT23/24.  The ring is therefore a property of the plant + the FCU's position loop, not of F1, F2 or
    of any phase-log rule.

(d) WHY THE TWO LONG LEGS AND NOT THE SHORT ONES: the outward half of the ring is 35-93 mm on every
    corner, but only the two 2.0 m legs have enough braking to make it large in E (their entry speed is
    1.69-1.75 m/s against 1.26-1.30 m/s on the 1.2 m legs), and only the legs whose travel direction is
    PERPENDICULAR to a wall can push the truth past it.  The four walls that carry the growth are exactly
    the four the arriving legs run along.

quantity: [next command - hold_s, next command], hold_s read from the run's own traj.log.  It is not used
to compute any millimetre of the growth, only to say WHEN an extreme happened.

== 4. THE CROSS-CHECK AGAINST B2: SOMETIMES THE SAME EVENT, USUALLY NOT
================================================================================
B2 and this file measure different things from the same truth: B2 is the worst perpendicular deviation
from the line through that leg phase's OWN endpoints (so it is blind to a uniform stand-off of the whole
leg, and it cannot see an overshoot along the direction of travel at all), while B1's growth is measured
against the COMMANDED walls.  The tool re-derives B2 with tools/metrics.py's own formula and prints, per
leg, the wall that leg runs along and the largest excursion past it, with a same/different verdict on the
timestamps (|dt| <= 0.3 s):

  RECT26 (the brief's "leg 2 41.3 mm" is RECT23's number; RECT26's leg 2 is 24.0 mm -- all three below):
      leg 2 (N=0.4 wall):  B2 24.0 mm at t 35.95   wall-out +29.4 mm at t 33.71   DIFFERENT
      leg 3 (E=2.5 wall):  B2 12.4 mm at t 48.28   wall-out +10.4 mm at t 45.53   DIFFERENT
      leg 4 (N=1.6 wall):  B2 22.2 mm at t 53.64   wall-out +19.1 mm at t 53.63   SAME EVENT
      leg 5 (E=0.5 wall):  B2 11.2 mm at t 67.49   wall-out  +8.2 mm at t 64.66   DIFFERENT
  RECT23:
      leg 2 (N=0.4 wall):  B2 41.3 mm at t 30.36   wall-out +60.7 mm at t 30.26   SAME EVENT (the departure dip)
      leg 4 (N=1.6 wall):  B2 16.2 mm at t 49.83   wall-out +19.4 mm at t 49.85   SAME EVENT
  RECT24: leg 2 B2 41.4 mm / wall-out +56.9 mm at t 30.45 SAME; leg 4 B2 13.0 mm / +42.9 mm different.

  SO: the B2 numbers are NOT the B1 growth, and on RECT26 they are mostly a different event -- B2's leg 2
  (24.0 mm) is a bulge 24 mm INSIDE the N-min wall at t 35.95, which cannot make B1 grow at all, while the
  E-max growth (35.1 mm) that B1 fails on is invisible to B2's perpendicular formula (it lies along the
  line).  Where they DO coincide is (i) the corner-arrival bulge seen across the leg's line -- RECT26 leg 4
  22.2 mm = the +19.1 mm past the N-max wall at c4 -- and (ii) the DEPARTURE DIP on RECT23/24/RECT7/9/10/11
  leg 2, where B2's 41.3/41.4 mm IS the outward dip (60.7/56.9 mm past the N-min wall) that the aircraft
  makes after leaving corner 1 at 1.5 m/s.  The departure dip is the one leg-side contributor to B1 in
  these sorties, and it is worth 29-61 mm on N-min depending on the run (RECT26: only ~29 mm, at the visit
  start; its N-min wall is instead owned by the closing corner 1 at 42.2 mm).

== 5. THE SLOW-ARM CONTROL: REMOVE THE BRAKING AND THE RING GOES AWAY
================================================================================
The same square, the same estimator, the same world, flown with params/phase_a_mission.parm (WP_SPD 0.25,
LOOP_ACC 0.3) as logs/missionA-stepB10-01-20261013_232331: B1 PASSES, growth E +38.4 mm and N +55.0 mm,
and its four wall extremes are no longer corner events at all -- they are mid-leg (0.065, 0.586, 0.203,
0.771 m from the nearest corner) at 0.043-0.315 m/s, and the N-max wall is never crossed (it reads
-42.2 mm, i.e. 42 mm INSIDE).  Ten of ten slow-arm sorties pass (docs/metrics/B1-adjudication-20261015.txt
section 4).  The ring is speed-dependent, which is what a position-loop ring is.

== 6. REFUTED, WITH THE NUMBERS THAT REFUTE THEM
================================================================================
  * the PAD / the entry leg: not in the window (section 0's rule opens at the arrival at corner 1), and
    the tool prints the entry visit separately, marked "BEFORE the B1 window".  On RECT26 the entry visit's
    own extreme is 500.4 mm of E at the pad -- i.e. if the entry leg WERE in the window the number would
    be ~2.5 m, not 2.110.
  * the FAST CORNER ARRIVAL as the F2 change defined it: the gate's crossing speed is now 0.083-0.151 m/s
    (measured at the closest approach) yet the ring's p-p is 136-217 mm and B1 barely moved.  What the F2
    change did not touch is the 1.26-1.75 m/s braking one metre out (section 2b) -- so the excitation
    survived the fix, and the 44 mm it did move is the part of the excursion that lived in the gate's own
    timing.
  * an ESTIMATOR excursion: at all four extremes the FCU's own estimate is 3.5-9.6 mm from the truth and
    the LIO is closer still (the parent unit measured 2-7 mm on RECT33/34); the FCU reports the same
    35-85 mm residual against its own commanded point.
  * a FLY-THROUGH: at all four extremes the truth speed is 0.002-0.012 m/s.  The aircraft stops out there.
  * B2 / straightness on the legs: a different quantity and, on RECT26, mostly a different event
    (section 4).
  * a STATIC OFFSET that waiting would remove: the settled position is within 5-18 mm of the commanded
    corner (the parent unit's RECT34 c2: peak +92.8 mm, settled +6.7 mm), while the ring's inner extreme
    is 99-142 mm on the other side.  Waiting does remove it; the 5 s dwell is simply not long enough for a
    ~4.3 s ring.

== 7. THE TWO CANDIDATE FIXES, WITH THEIR EXPECTED EFFECT AND THEIR COST
================================================================================
Both are OUTSIDE this round's authority (no flight, no bound change, no shared-file edit); they are listed
with the millimetres that support them and what each would cost to prove.

  (1) PLAN-SIDE APPROACH SHAPING -- do not present a 2.0 m position STEP to the FCU.  Split each leg into
      a cruise segment and a short final approach whose setpoint MOVES (or carry a velocity
      feed-forward), so the vehicle arrives at the corner with a deceleration profile instead of a step
      response.  Expected effect from section 2: the ring's excitation is the 1.26-1.75 m/s braking, so
      the E walls' 35-93 mm and the N walls' 32-71 mm should fall to the slow arm's scale (+24.2/+14.2 on
      E, i.e. -74 and -78 % on RECT26's growth); the FCU estimate's own 50-100 mm arrival tail would not
      be removed by this, so the honest expectation is "B1 passes with margin", not "zero".
      Cost: it changes what the plan box publishes, so every B1/B2/B3 number and the F1/F2 acceptance
      re-verification have to be re-run (RECT23/24/26/33/34 and the two kill-plan sorties), plus new unit
      tests for the stream's shape (tools/test-cmd-stream.py).

  (2) FCU-SIDE POSITION-LOOP GAINS -- damping the ring at the plant, with no plan change: PSC_XY/VELXY
      gains (nothing in params/ overrides them today; params/phase_a_mission*.parm only set WP_*/LOIT_*/
      PSC_ACCZ_P) and/or the LOIT_* limits, which is exactly the knob the slow arm turns from the other
      end (LOIT_SPEED_MS 2.0 -> 0.25, LOIT_ACC_MAX_M 3.0 -> 0.3: B1 goes from FAIL to +38.4/+55.0 mm).
      Expected effect: a ring of 136-217 mm p-p at ~0.23 Hz implies a loop that is under-damped at that
      frequency; raising the velocity-loop damping (or lowering the position-loop bandwidth) trades
      settling speed for overshoot directly, and the ring's outer half is the entire B1 growth.
      Cost: it is a FLIGHT-CONTROLLER change, so it needs a bench/step-response measurement before any
      sortie (the project's own rule: a gain change must be shown on a step response, not inferred from a
      mission metric), plus a re-run of A1-A4 and B3 to show the tracking accuracy did not regress.  It
      also changes a parameter file that every existing number in docs/ was measured with, so the frozen
      mission-A set and the parameter provenance lines in docs/ have to be updated deliberately.

  A third, cheaper, PLAN-side variant is already supported by the data but is NOT a fix for B1: shortening
  the dwell would not help (the ring decays through it), and enlarging the corner tolerance would only
  move where the ring is centred.
