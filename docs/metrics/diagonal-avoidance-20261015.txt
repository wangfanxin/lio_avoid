THE DIAGONAL: an obstacle-avoidance test that the CONTROL passes and the TEST fails
2026-10-15 15:28:50Z   HEAD: 7cfe723

=== WHY A NEW MISSION WAS NEEDED ==============================================================
Every existing world's tour threads between pillars with room to spare, MEASURED offline by
_stage/pick-avoidance-mission.py (minimum segment clearance to a pillar SURFACE):
   forest_all.yaml +0.428 m | pillars.yaml +0.600 | corridor_fast +0.700 | forest6x4 +0.962
   | pillars_fast +1.114
So a planner that ignores obstacles and a planner that avoids them fly the same path, and no
avoidance can be demonstrated.  The owner's 'diagonal' is the leg that CROSSES the field, so the
mission was defined for that purpose: worlds/diagonal.yaml.

=== THE MISSION, CHOSEN SO THE STRAIGHT LINE IS BLOCKED ======================================
Pillars and arena are IDENTICAL to worlds/pillars.yaml (46 pillars r=0.30, arena +-5 m, 0.0-4.2 m
tall); only the waypoints differ.  Chosen by search (_stage/write-diagonal-world2.py) with two
constraints at once: every WAYPOINT clear of every pillar, every LEG passing through one.
   corners (W): (-4.00 -4.00 2.0)  and  (-1.00 -1.00 2.0)
   waypoint clearance : +0.407 m each   (a place the vehicle can be)
   leg 1 clearance    : -0.300 m        (through a pillar)
   leg 2 clearance    : -0.300 m        (through a pillar)

=== DIAG1-rect-control: THE CONTROL, AND IT BEHAVES AS PREDICTED =============================
The rectangle plan box has NO pillar awareness -- its only mention of the world is its --world
default ( finds nothing).
PREDICTED BEFORE FLYING: C2 must catch it.  MEASURED:
   A1 route horizontal rms      5.89 mm   (target <= 20)
   A2 route vertical rms        2.83 mm   (target <= 50, mean removed)
   C2 obstacle clearance over the whole flight (46 pillars from diagonal.yaml): minimum pillar-surface distance -0.299 m;  9635 of 54929 samples inside the 0.20 m footprint  [FAIL]
   C1 final point: the aircraft stopped 0.028 m from the last commanded point (-4.000, -4.000)  [PASS]
   the truth's worst approach: -0.299 m;  9635 of 54929 samples inside the 0.20 m footprint (10.6%)
   the straight line it was asked to fly: 52.8% and 70.8% of the two legs inside the footprint
   rc=0, hung=0, no abort -- the sortie is valid, and it is INVALID as flight quality because it
   flew through a pillar.  That is the point: this is the negative control for the C2 instrument.

=== WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH =====================================================
ESTABLISHES: (a) the instrument works -- a planner with no obstacle awareness is caught by C2, so a
PASS on this mission would have meant something; (b) ego, as wired by us, did NOT avoid; (c) both
  planners can complete this mission, so the failure is not an inability to fly it.
DOES NOT ESTABLISH WHY.  The candidates, in the order they should be checked, and none of them is
yet measured:
  1. DID EGO'S MAP EVER SEE THE PILLARS?  Its map is built from /ego/cloud, i.e. OUR relay of
     /lio/cloud_world.  Nothing in this round recorded ego's occupancy (grid_map/occupancy_inflate)
     or the map's own view, so 'the map was empty where the pillars are' and 'the map had them and
     the planner ignored them' are indistinguishable from these logs.  THIS IS THE NEXT MEASUREMENT,
     and it is why criterion 3 names that topic explicitly.
  2. the LIO's registered cloud may be SPARSE on pillars: an offline check on a 3-scan recording
     found 10 of 46 pillars with NO returns at all (_stage/ego1-pad-obstacle-test.py), which is
     expected from one viewpoint but has never been measured over a whole crossing;
  3. the relay's 0.10 m decimation is exactly the map's resolution, so it cannot thin a pillar away
     -- but the relay also CROPS to +-5.5 m around the vehicle, and the far waypoint is 5.66 m away;
  4. ego's collision check samples only the first 2/3 of a trajectory's duration and its sample
     spacing is displacement/resolution (both read from bspline_opt, not measured here).
