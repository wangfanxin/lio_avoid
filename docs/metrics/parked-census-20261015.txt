=== FIRST, THE REFUTATION, BECAUSE IT IS THE MORE IMPORTANT RESULT =============================
The previous round reported "a HALF-SPACE SPLIT THROUGH THE VEHICLE" -- 23 of 46 pillars in ego's map,
essentially all with x > 0, and 15 dropped, essentially all with x < 0.  THAT WAS AN ARTEFACT OF MY OWN TOOL,
and the check that found it took one offline script and no flight.

  The census compared the occupied cells it sampled against world["pillars"].  The cells are published by
  EGO'S MAP, which works in L; the world file is in W.  The two differ by the project's own rotation, and
  the census did not apply it.

  HOW WRONG THAT IS, measured (_stage/inspect-field-symmetry.py): the field is a 10x10 lattice at 0.5 m with
  54 points removed, and it is symmetric under 180 deg (44 of 46 pillars map onto a pillar) but NOT under
  90 deg (0 of 46).  So an unconverted comparison shares NO positions with the truth and every "match" is a
  coincidence.  The counts it printed were not pillar counts, and the split was the shape of the coincidence.
  The transform itself was never in doubt: inv(R_WL) and the manual (N, -E) agree to the calibration's tilt.

  THE REPAIR is in src/tools/map-census.py: the pillars are converted W -> L with the same common/frames.py
  the flights use, the tool prints the conversion, and it EXITS NON-ZERO if the calibration cannot be read --
  because comparing across frames yields plausible-looking numbers that mean nothing, which is what happened.

=== AND WITH THE FRAME FIXED, A PATTERN SURVIVES -- A DIFFERENT ONE, AND NOW IN L ==============
Every pillar ego's map drops, in the frame the map itself works in:

      index 10  W(E-2.50 N-4.50)  ->  L(-4.50 +2.50)      index 24  W(E+0.50 N-3.50)  ->  L(-3.50 -0.50)
      index 11  W(E-2.50 N-2.50)  ->  L(-2.50 +2.50)      index 32  W(E+2.50 N-3.50)  ->  L(-3.50 -2.50)
      index 15  W(E-1.50 N-3.50)  ->  L(-3.50 +1.50)      index 36  W(E+3.50 N-4.50)  ->  L(-4.50 -3.50)
      index 20  W(E-0.50 N-4.50)  ->  L(-4.50 +0.50)      index 37  W(E+3.50 N-2.50)  ->  L(-2.50 -3.50)
      index 21  W(E-0.50 N-2.50)  ->  L(-2.50 +0.50)      index 41  W(E+4.50 N-3.50)  ->  L(-3.50 -4.50)

   L_x of the dropped set: {-2.50, -3.50, -4.50}.  L_x of the kept set: everything > -2.50.  L_y ranges over
   +2.5 ... -4.5 across the dropped set, so this is NOT a y split.
   SO: A HARD CUT AT L_x = -2.5, and NOT through the vehicle, whose own position is the L origin.

   The one candidate that produces a hard cut in x is the map's acceptance test -- `grid_map.cpp:851-855`
   takes a cloud point only when it lies within `grid_map/local_update_range` OF THE VEHICLE, per axis.  A
   band of half-width 5.5 cut at -2.5 implies `camera_pos_.x = +3.0`; other combinations of range and offset
   give the same cut.  THAT IS A HYPOTHESIS with one number in it, and this round does NOT test it: the next
   one reads the parameters the node actually loaded and, if that is not enough, the map's own position.

=== WHAT IS ESTABLISHED, AND WHAT IS NOT ======================================================
ESTABLISHED: (1) our relay is faithful -- 0 pillars lost between the LIO and ego, in BOTH runs;
  (2) ego's map does lose pillars -- 9 or 10 of the 38 it was handed, and about 62% of the distinct occupied
  cells its input carried (a ratio INDEPENDENT of the frame bug, since both sides of that comparison are
  counts of cells);
  (3) WITH the frame fixed there is a hard cut at L_x = -2.5 -- and the first report of a cut THROUGH THE
  VEHICLE was wrong.
NOT ESTABLISHED: the mechanism.  No flight this round; the cut is a measurement, the cause is not.
STILL A LIMIT OF THE INSTRUMENT: the census is z-BLIND (a 2-D union of x,y), so a cell counted as "on the
  pillar" could come from another height.  The three-stage comparison is unaffected -- all three stages were
  censused the same way -- but a per-pillar claim is not a 3-D statement.
