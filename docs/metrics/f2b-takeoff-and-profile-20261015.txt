F2b -- THE TAKE-OFF CONTRACT, SETTLED BY BENCH, AND THE OWNER'S 0.2 m PROFILE
================================================================================
Written 2026-10-15 by the round that applied the take-off fix.  Commits 3f95878 (the fix) and fd974c0 (the
owner's profile + the two flight-path defects it exposed).  Every number below is copied from the tool or
from the FCU's own BIN that produced it; the run directory is named next to each block.


1. THE DEFECT, AND THE ONE NUMBER THAT WAS WRONG
------------------------------------------------
`bridge/mavlink_impl.py::takeoff` sent `datum + alt_m` -- 2.50 m became 586.59 m with the origin at 584 m --
to satisfy the guard in `ArduCopter/takeoff.cpp:29`, `takeoff_alt_m <= copter.current_loc.alt * 0.01`, read
as an AMSL-scale comparison.  The SAME number goes to the executor, `Mode::_TakeOff::start_m`
(`takeoff.cpp:51-57`), which computes `take_off_complete_alt_m = get_pos_estimate_U_m() + alt_m`: it ADDS
what it is given.  The command therefore asked GUIDED for ~586 m above home.

THE GUARD NEVER NEEDED IT.  `current_loc.alt` is itself alt-above-home (`ArduCopter/inertia.cpp:24-30` sets
exactly that), so `* 0.01` compares metres with metres; on the pad the threshold is ~0.002 m and 2.50 passes
with room to spare.  THE OLD MEASUREMENT'S CONDITION: the docstring used to record a 2026-10-13 reading, a
0.8 m request answering MAV_RESULT_FAILED with no statustext.  That was taken BEFORE the EKF origin existed
(`set_ekf_origin` did not exist yet); with no origin `ModeGuided::do_user_takeoff_start_m` cannot convert the
altitude from ABOVE_HOME to ABOVE_ORIGIN, which is the failure that method now documents itself.  A real
failure, attributed to the wrong cause.  The request now goes out VERBATIM and the datum is only logged.

3. THE OWNER'S DESIGN (2026-10-15), IN THE CODE AND PRINTED AT START-UP
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
"起飞命令就飞20cm高，然后立马进定点".  TWO MECHANISMS, BOTH PRESENT, THE FLOWN ONE STATED:
  * `--takeoff-command-m` (default 0.2) = what goes out as `packet.z`, verbatim;
  * `--trigger-on-airborne` = the go goes out the moment the vehicle is airborne;
  * `rectangle_source.py --vertical-first-m <mission altitude> --vertical-hold-s 3.0` = the plan's first
    target is a single-point leg STRAIGHT UP from the pad, and its 3.0 s dwell IS the A4 window -- which is
    what keeps A4 a hover at a known point instead of a climbing transit;
  * `--climb-guard-m 1.0` stays ON, against the MISSION altitude, so a 0.2 m command cannot trip it;
  * setting `--takeoff-command-m` equal to `--alt` restores the other mechanism (the FCU's own controller
    flies the whole climb).  `tools/run-mission.sh` prints `take-off : FCU-TARGET ...` or
    `take-off : SETPOINT-DRIVEN CLIMB (the owner's design) ...`, and `supervisor.py` prints its own line.

MEASURED ON THE BENCH, NOT ASSUMED: `clean 0.2` ACCEPTED, `landed_state 2`; `clean_sp 0.2 2.0` climbs and
holds (above).

WHAT THE FLIGHT EXPOSED, AND IT IS OUR OWN FLIGHT PATH, NOT THE FIRMWARE.  The vertical climb leg did not
run as designed, and the record of the three attempts IS the evidence:
  * `RECT31-accept`: the plan box's arrival test is HORIZONTAL, so the climb leg "arrived" while the aircraft
    was still over the pad -- `ARRIVED at waypoint 1: the vehicle is 0.004 m from it ... after 0.0 s`, with
    the next log line reporting `altitude 0.111 m`.  The climb never appeared in the command stream and
    corner 1's 2.0 m became the first setpoint.  The phase log has no `leg1` at all.
  * the first fix compared the leg against `target_w`, which a few lines above is `self.target_w[:2]` -- so
    the test became "is the target's X and Y zero".  Caught on a REPLAY of `RECT31`'s own recorded odometry
    through the module (`_stage/f2b-replay-rect31.py`), which is what settled it: the printed line showed
    `target_w=[0. 0.]` and the 2-D branch still taken.
  * the second fix used an exact-zero extent test, which classified the leg as horizontal BY 0.9 mm (the
    measured calibration puts the pad at (-0.9, -4.2) mm in the vehicle's frame).
  * `RECT32-accept` then used the full 3-D distance and the leg TIMED OUT: `LEG 1 TIMEOUT: ... it is 1.831 m
    from the commanded waypoint 1 after 20.0 s`.  The horizontal term was contributing centimetres of drift
    that a climb straight up from the pad is not asked to remove.
  THE RULE NOW: a leg whose own endpoints share a horizontal position within `arrive_m / 10` is VERTICAL and
  is judged on the HEIGHT -- a climb's question is "has the climb finished" -- with
  `vertical_arrive_slack_m` (0.20 m) on top of the gate, because this stack's altitude datum carries a
  measured ~0.1 m term (`t_WL_z` = +0.1156 m; A4's own datum line reads D -0.098 m in flight).  Every leg
  with horizontal extent keeps the horizontal test UNCHANGED, which is what every row above and every
  sortie in `docs/metrics/` was scored against.  The decision is printed once per leg.
  `RECT33-accept` is the sortie that MEASURED THE LEG ACTUALLY FLYING: the wire's own first row is
  `traj_id 1  p_w=(0.00000, 0.00000, 2.50000)  mask=2552`, i.e. the vertical target reached the FCU first,
  and the plan's own line reads `LEG 1 arrival rule: TESTING the HEIGHT (this leg has no horizontal extent);
  height error 2.3872 m, gate 0.260 m`.  The leg then hit its 20 s TIMEOUT with `height error 1.817 m`: the
  aircraft did not climb to 2.44 m within the leg's 20 s, so the climb leg is COMMANDED AND JUDGED
  CORRECTLY BUT NOT YET FLOWN TO ITS ARRIVAL.  The aircraft still reached the mission altitude (Alt +2.332 m)
  and every acceptance row passed, because corner 1's own setpoint took it there.

WHAT THIS MEANS, STATED PLAINLY RATHER THAN WORKED AROUND: the 0.2 m command, the airborne trigger, the
vertical target, the height-based arrival and the climb guard are all in place and all measured; what is NOT
yet demonstrated is the vertical leg ARRIVING INSIDE ITS OWN TIMEOUT, and with it the intended shape of the
A4 window (a hover at the top of the climb).  On all three sorties A4 passed and the aircraft hovered at the
mission altitude -- but it got there on corner 1's setpoint, not by the climb leg completing.  The next
attempt should give the climb leg a timeout of its own (`--leg-timeout-s` is 20 s for every leg today) or
raise the vertical slack, and it must be flown before the climb-leg shape is claimed.


5. THE SECOND DEFECT: THE ABORT LANDED NOTHING
-----------------------------------------------
`RECT27-kill-plan` (the plan box killed 8 s into the route) produced the NAMED abort and then exited 0.05 s
later:
    [  28.94] bridge/mavlink: mode -> LAND
    [  28.94] final: armed=True mode=LAND alt AMSL 586.0 m
and the FCU's own log for that sortie ends with the aircraft STILL ARMED AT 2.002 m (no MODE 9 until
t 41.72; the FCU was still in mode 4 when its log closed).  The abort had taken over the MODE and let go of
the AIRCRAFT.  TWO causes, both measured on the next attempt:
  * `pump()` returns the moment `abort_reason` is set, and the abort's landing pumps THROUGH it;
  * `check_gates()` SETS THE REASON AGAIN during the landing -- the plan-silence gate that fired the abort is
    still true while the aircraft comes down -- so `RECT27b` failed identically with the reason explicitly
    cleared first.  The abort's parking brake was switching itself back on.
THE FIX: the abort lands through `self.land()` (commands LAND, lets the commanded mode take effect, waits for
the FCU's OWN disarm, writes the `land` phase, logs `landed: armed=...`), and `check_gates` is disarmed once
the sortie is ending (`gates_armed`, cleared in `finish`).  The landing's own "still armed" verdict is
deliberately discarded so the NAMED reason survives into the summary.

VERIFIED, `RECT27d-kill-plan`:
    phases: preflight takeoff hold leg1 leg2 abort land done
    [  29.29] ABORT: THE PLAN BOX HAS STOPPED TALKING: nothing on /plan/cmd for 3.0 s (limit 3.0 s) after
              589 messages, trajectory_id 2 -- ... SILENCE IS NOT COMPLETION
    [  36.58] landed: armed=False mode=LAND
    [  36.58] final: armed=False mode=LAND alt AMSL 584.2 m

    FCU's own BIN: MODE 9 (LAND) at t 41.75; ARM event t 48.12 ArmState 0 (DISARMED)
  New test cases I and I' in `tools/test-stall-gate.py` (the gates must NOT re-fire while the sortie is
  ending; the negative control shows the same condition DOES abort with the gates live).

7. TWO LINES ON THE INTERFACE, AS ASKED
----------------------------------------
  (a) The wire mask's `pos` is NOT a preference: `planner/cmd_stream.py::sample` publishes
      `position = the waypoint in hand, velocity = 0, acceleration = 0`, so mask 2552 (POS_YAW) is the only
      thing we have to send -- there is no velocity field to send.
  (b) CONSEQUENCE: the straight-line motion AND the whole speed profile between waypoints are produced by
      the FCU's position controller, not by us.  Any future attempt to shape an approach -- slowing before a
      corner, which B1 may need -- must be done by ADDING WAYPOINTS or by sending a non-zero velocity field
      DELIBERATELY, and must not assume a velocity feed-forward exists today.  Equally: a vertical first
      target plus four corners is not, by itself, sufficient to shape the take-off -- measured above.
