WHY IT STILL COLLIDES -- THREE INDEPENDENT AUDITS, AND THE ANSWER IS TWO HALVES, NEITHER OF THEM THE PLANNER
(2026-10-16, no flight; every number below is measured in `logs/missionA-FLIGHTA-routeb-hold`)

THE QUESTION.  The aircraft entered a pillar for 0.33 s (0.42 % of the samples, minimum clearance -0.136 m) on a
run whose mission completed and landed normally.  Three independent audits were run -- planner/FSM, command path,
perception/map -- each given only the data pipeline and the symptom, with no access to the others' findings.  I
also measured the plan-vs-truth comparison myself.  This file is the synthesis, including the two places where
the audits corrected me.

WHAT IS *NOT* THE CAUSE (all measured, each would have been a plausible story)
  * **Not the plan.**  Reconstructing all 30 committed splines from `traj_server`'s own `NEW-SPLINE` records and
    measuring each over its WHOLE duration against all 46 pillars: minimum clearance **+0.0480 m**, and **zero of
    30 plans below 0**.  The plan in force at the strike (`traj_id 22`, committed sim 56.468, 3.300 s) had its
    own minimum at **+0.1668 m** and its clearance where it was being flown at **+0.495 m**.  The planner's own
    per-plan column agrees (min +0.0480 m over 4 895 rows, none below 0), and so does the independent set
    (`traj.csv`, 100 Hz, segment-aware).
  * **Not the wire, and this was checked AT THE FLIGHT CONTROLLER.**  Three records of the commanded point agree:
    `setpoints.csv` cmd_* (min +0.0483 m, **0 inside**), the bridge's `wire.csv`, and -- the decisive one --
    **`flight.BIN` message GUIP ("Guided mode position target"), which is what the FCU itself received**: min
    +0.0483 m, **0 inside**, and GUIP row 1 equals wire.csv row 1 to 1e-6 m.  (The FCU's flight log is a new
    instrument for this project and it settles a question the audits had listed as open.)
  * **Not the estimate.**  Across the intrusion window the FCU's own EKF estimate is 0.020-0.065 m from the truth
    (at sim 57.20: fcu-truth = (+0.041, -0.006) m while the command was 0.658 m away in N).
  * **Not the map.**  ego's own DIAG/2 occupancy slice one second BEFORE the strike (sim 56.468) has the pillar
    at W(-2.5,-4.5) marked (20 of 29 cells within 0.31 m of its axis read '1', 18 of 19 pillar axes in the slice
    marked), and **477 of the 600 truth samples of the strike window (79.5 %) fall in cells the planner's map
    already marked OCCUPIED**.  The optimiser also refused 183 candidate plans from that neighbourhood in sim
    57.499-57.876 with "First 3 control points in obstacles".
  * **Not the invalid-time branch** (0 INVALID-TIME lines; every spline arrived with `age_at_arrival=0.000`).

HALF ONE -- WE THROW THE PLAN'S ACCELERATION AWAY, AND THE FLIGHT CONTROLLER THEN FLIES ITS OWN REFERENCE MODEL
  The wire carries `type_mask` **2496** = position + velocity + yaw, with **AFX/AFY/AFZ set to literal zero**
  (`bridge/mavlink_impl.py:55`; `BRIDGE_FIELDS=posvel`).  In ArduPilot 4.7.1 that path is
  `GCS_MAVLink_Copter.cpp:1094-1098` -> `mode_guided.cpp:925-943` -> `AC_PosControl::input_pos_vel_accel_NE_m`,
  which runs the position through `shape_pos_vel_accel_xy` (`AP_Math/control.cpp:346-400`): **our position is only
  a GOAL for an acceleration-limited reference model**, our velocity is added unshaped, and the acceleration is
  limited to `WPNAV_ACCELERATION_MS` (2.5 m/s2 -- realised 2.463 m/s2, measured from PSCN).

  What the plan asks for, and what the FCU will produce:
      the plan's own acceleration .......... p90 3.63, p99 6.15, max 13.84 m/s2   (`setpoints.csv` acc_*)
      the wire's 0.2 s-smoothed |a| ........ p99 4.69, max 5.77 m/s2
      the FCU's reference realises ........ 2.463 m/s2                            (max |d2ref/dt2| from PSCN)
      12.4 % of wire samples (N axis) demand > 2.0 m/s2 sustained over 0.2 s.

  So the FCU's internal reference trails the wire -- and **the vehicle follows its own reference, not the wire**.
  That is not an interpretation: `PSCN.TVN - PSCN.DVN = PSC_NE_POS_P x (DPN - PN)` holds to **0.000000 m/s** over
  330 samples on both axes (`PSC_NE_POS_P = 1.0` from the BIN's PARM), i.e. the loop closes on `_pos_desired`
  (`AC_PosControl.cpp:721-723`).  At sim 57.075:

      WIRE setpoint (msg 84)  ............ W(-2.616, -3.808, 1.924)   clearance **+0.4020 m**
      FCU internal reference ............ W(-2.625, -4.280, 1.927)   clearance **-0.0470 m**  <- inside
      FCU position estimate (= truth) ... W(-2.616, -4.399, 1.927)   clearance **-0.1460 m**
      wire->ref 0.4725 m | ref->pos 0.1193 m | wire->pos 0.5914 m

  Over the flight: |truth - wire| median 0.052 / p90 0.348 / max 0.691 m, while **|FCU-reference - wire| median
  0.066 / p90 0.287 / max 0.591 m** and |truth - FCU-reference| median 0.046 / p90 0.160 / max 0.263 m.  **0.47 of
  the 0.59 m gap is manufactured inside ArduPilot, before the control loop runs.**

HALF TWO -- THE REFERENCE IS DISCONTINUOUS, NOTHING BOUNDS IT, AND THE FSM WATCHES THE PLAN INSTEAD OF THE VEHICLE
  * **The 1 Hz sawtooth, in the air**: `t_cur = now - start_time` (`traj_server.cpp:195`) resets to ~0 when a new
    spline arrives (`:62`), and the new spline is anchored on the vehicle's estimate -- so the sampled reference
    teleports.  Measured on the wire: **14 of 16 replans step more than 0.05 m, median 0.281 m, max 0.927 m**, and
    the tracking error collapses from a median 0.234 m just before a replan to 0.022 m just after.  The consequence
    is structural: **the error is RESET at each replan and then has the whole spline interval to grow** -- the
    intrusion is 1.24 s after the 55.897 reset, and no replan came until 57.896.
  * **The excitation before this intrusion**: a single 10 ms `/plan/cmd` message stepping **0.4030 m** where
    0.0158 m was due (25x, 28 deg direction change, |v| 1.55 -> 0.38 m/s) at sim 55.910.  It came from one of two
    **sub-second commits** (gaps 0.617 s and 0.549 s at 55.919 and 56.468) which the FSM's own replan gate cannot
    produce (every `EXEC_TRAJ -> REPLAN_TRAJ` path requires `t_cur > 1.0 s`; the goal did not change;
    EMERGENCY_STOP never fired).  The only remaining silent commit path is
    `checkCollisionCallback -> planFromCurrentTraj` (`ego_replan_fsm.cpp:1236`), whose success prints nothing --
    attributed by elimination, not by a logged reason.  The vehicle had been commanded a south-east acceleration
    on the replaced plan, carried that lateral velocity through the reset (velocity lag 0.36 s E / 0.42 s N), and
    drifted.
  * **The drift is CROSS-TRACK, not a lag**: at the worst sample the truth is 0.499 m to the side of the plan and
    only 0.173 m behind it, and the cross-track component grows monotonically 0.002 -> 0.499 m over 56.0 ->
    57.14 s while the along-track one saturates.  The plan's clearance where it was flying was +0.495 m, so the
    aircraft overran the plan's own margin by 4 mm.
  * **The FSM never reacted.** `checkCollisionCallback` evaluates `info->position_traj_.evaluateDeBoorT(...)`
    (`ego_replan_fsm.cpp:1201`, `:1213`) -- the PLAN -- and `odom_pos_` is never compared with the map anywhere in
    the FSM.  Counters for the whole run: `Suddenly discovered obstacles` **0**, `current traj in collision,
    replan` **0**, `Depth Lost` **0**, emergency_stop 0 -- while the vehicle sat inside a pillar for 0.33 s and the
    next commit came 0.95 s later.
  * **Nothing bounds the commanded point's distance from the vehicle.**  `traj_server` has no odometry
    subscription at all (`traj_server.cpp:289-306`); the shell's `jump_m` is recorded and never acted on; the
    bridge sends the newest sample with no comparison to the last.  The only distance test in the stack is the
    shell's arrival gate, which sequences the MISSION.

AND THE MARGIN THE PLAN GIVES ITSELF IS 5-17 cm
  Nothing in this optimiser prefers the middle of free space: `dyn_a_star.cpp:216` is
  `static_cost = sqrt(dx^2+dy^2+dz^2)` and the file is BYTE-IDENTICAL to its `.pre-clearance1` backup, i.e. the
  clearance experiment is not in flight.  The only obstacle term is `calcDistanceCostRebound`, which is exactly
  zero until `check_collision_and_rebound` has filled `cps_.direction[]`, and its target is 0.5 m from the chord's
  entry point, not a real obstacle distance.  Also structural: `check_collision_and_rebound` scans only the first
  ~two thirds of the control points (`bspline_optimizer.cpp:1319-1326`), `rebound_optimize`'s acceptance test
  samples only `t < 2/3` of the duration (`:1638`), and the second clearance check is `#ifdef`'d out (`:1663`) --
  and **12 of the 30 plans have their tightest point at tau > 2/3**, including the two tightest of the flight.
  Two further findings worth their own line: an occupied control point is re-classified FREE if any accumulated
  `base_point`/`direction` projects to less than one cell (`:1334-1343`), and when its A* fails the segment is
  ERASED and the function still returns TRUE (`:1403-1409`, `:1510-1511`), i.e. that segment gets no repulsion at
  all.  A* also exhausts its 100^3 pool (56 times in this run, all on the pad) and its start-escape loop marches
  the start away from the goal with no cap (`dyn_a_star.cpp:96-105`).

WHAT WOULD HAVE PREVENTED THIS SPECIFIC INTRUSION, IN ORDER OF LEVERAGE
  1. **Make the FSM's safety check look at the VEHICLE** -- `odom_pos_` against the map, next to the plan check it
     already does.  A handful of lines, and it would have reacted inside the 0.33 s instead of 0.95 s later.  (This
     is also the one check whose absence made "0 counter fired" possible.)
  2. **Tag the commit reason** on the existing plan-start line, so the sub-second silent replans are visible, and
     re-commit from odometry when a plan expires (`traj_server.cpp:214-225` currently commands the spline's END
     with v = 0 -- a full-authority "go there" at whatever distance; 8 silences > 1.2 s, max 7.26 s).
  3. **Do not send the flight controller an acceleration it must discard, or clamp what we send.**  Restoring the
     feed-forward (`posvelacc`) is the direct fix for half one, but it was refuted by DIAG33 -- and the reason is
     half two: with the age-rule sawtooth in place, the acceleration we send alternates at 22 m/s2.  So the order
     matters: continuity first (review step 5), then `posvelacc`; or clamp the acceleration to the shaper's
     ceiling (2.5 m/s2) and keep `posvel`.
  4. **Review step 5** -- re-anchor the sampled point onto the vehicle, keep it monotone, look 0.5 s ahead:
     removes the median 0.281 m / max 0.927 m reference steps and the error-reset behaviour.
  5. **Give the plan margin** -- an A* proximity term and a real distance cost -- and **check the last third** of
     every spline.  This is a margin fix, not this run's cause: with a 0.5 m tracking error, no margin below 0.5 m
     is enough on its own.

TWO CORRECTIONS THE AUDITS MADE TO MY OWN ANALYSIS (both mine, both recorded)
  * I attributed the excursion to a lag (the commanded speed median 1.34 m/s against the truth's 0.79 m/s).  The
    decomposition says **cross-track 0.499 m, along-track -0.173 m** -- a lateral drift, not a lag -- and the
    excitation is the 0.403 m reference discontinuity, not a speed shortfall.
  * I reported the commanded stream as 3.55 % inside a pillar and a command 6.58 m from the vehicle: both were my
    own frame error on `setpoints.csv` (see the correction appended to `collision-forensics-20261016.txt`).

=== THE THIRD AUDIT (perception and frames), AND THE CONVERGED ANSWER ====================================

The perception/map audit ran the same way -- given only the pipeline and the symptom -- and it also clears its
own layer, while adding one defect nobody had found:

* **The map held the pillar, and its marks sit exactly where the aircraft was.**  In the DIAG/2 slice nearest the
  impact (t = 57.58, 0.44 s after): 57 occupied cells within 0.70 m of that pillar's axis, 19 of the 27 cells
  inside its true footprint -- and the truth path's minimum distance to any MAP MARK over the whole flight is
  **0.000 m at t = 57.14**, i.e. there was a marked cell exactly where the aircraft was.  (The 1 Hz slice rate
  brackets the impact at -0.66 / +0.44 s; that coincident mark is the only exactly-timed datum.)
* **Where the map thought the vehicle was**: its pose is the FCU's EKF estimate in L, L(-4.344, 2.517, 1.816),
  against the truth's L(-4.348, 2.578, 1.719) -- 44 mm horizontal, 105 mm vertical -- and that put the map's own
  vehicle 0.151 m from the pillar's axis, i.e. **0.149 m inside the surface and inside the map's own marked band,
  so its own cell read OCCUPIED**.
* **NEW DEFECT, its own fix: the map's pillar is a HOLLOW ANNULUS.**  Along the vehicle's approach ray the first
  mark appears at **0.06-0.18 m from the axis** (median 0.10) although the true surface is at 0.30 m, and the last
  at 0.30-0.72 m (median 0.52).  So the map leaves free space up to ~0.20 m INSIDE the pillar and makes the
  effective obstacle ~0.20 m fatter outside; the corridor between two pillars 1.414 m apart is only about 0.4 m
  wide in map terms.  **Why marks reach inside is UNRESOLVED**: the only cloud sample recorded is clean (radial p05
  0.288 m, peak 0.28-0.33 m) and `/ego/cloud` was not recorded at the impact -- re-running with the relay's output
  at 5 Hz settles it.
* **The map marks the vehicle itself**: the pad top and the virtual-ceiling slab (z in [2.90, 3.00)) both produce
  marks under the camera, which is where `the drone is in obstacle` 4346 comes from (4165 of them during the climb
  off the pad, 173 at t 60.7-61.3 s).
* **Two instrument facts**: `diag_msgs` is declared and printed but never incremented, so `msgs=0` in every DIAG/1
  line is a DEAD COUNTER and not "no cloud arrived" (points reached 3.6 M); and the stdout(DIAG)/stderr(rclcpp)
  interleave carries a few-tenths-of-a-second ordering bias, so DIAG times must be fitted (median residual 43 mm).
* **The relay is cleared**: the 8 s union does not smear a pillar along the direction of travel (static world, LIO
  pose 7.7 mm rms against the truth); it costs 1.6-10.7x in point count; it crashed at teardown (t ~75.5 s, the
  landing), not at the encounter.
* And it names the frame trap explicitly for future audits: `setpoints.csv` has `cmd_*` and `fcu_*` in W but
  `lio_*` in L, and taken at face value it reports a phantom 5.81 m rms error and a phantom 448 ms lag.

  So the answer to "why does it still collide" is NOT one bug.  The aircraft was flown 0.499 m to the side of a
  clean plan by a reference that (a) is generated as a wall-clock function of a spline rather than of the
  vehicle's progress, (b) is discontinuous at every replan and occasionally steps 0.4-0.9 m, and (c) is then
  handed to a flight controller whose own acceleration-limited reference model trails it by a further 0.47 m
  because we zero the acceleration field; while the only safety check in the loop watches the plan, not the
  aircraft, and the plan gives itself 5-17 cm of margin.

  The single most cost-effective change is also the smallest: **make the FSM's collision check test `odom_pos_`
  against the map, next to the plan check it already does.**  It would have reacted inside the 0.33 s instead of
  0.95 s later, and its absence is the reason every safety counter read zero while the aircraft was inside a
  pillar.
