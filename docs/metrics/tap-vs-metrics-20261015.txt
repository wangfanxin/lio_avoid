== 2. WHY: the reader ran while the writers were still writing
================================================================================
tools/run-mission.sh stopped the FSM, computed the metrics, and exited -- and only THEN did its EXIT trap
stop SITL and the recorders.  So tools/metrics.py read tap.csv while sim/truth_rx.py was still in its
receive loop and while SITL was still appending the FCU's BIN.

A PASSIVE WATCHER MEASURED IT (_stage/s21-watch.py read tap.csv every 100 ms during RECT13-watch and did
not touch the flight; its whole log is _stage/s21-watch.log):
   10:40:49.982166  tap  61856 rows   truth_report -        metrics.txt -
   10:40:50.528632  tap  62516 rows   truth_report -        metrics.txt -
   10:40:51.488642  tap  63664 rows   truth_report -        metrics.txt WRITTEN
   10:40:52.601703  tap  64970 rows   truth_report present  metrics.txt WRITTEN
metrics.txt was written while the tap held ~62500 rows; the recorder went on to write 64970.

================================================================================
== 3. THE FIRST FIX WAS NECESSARY AND NOT SUFFICIENT -- a measurement, not an opinion
================================================================================
The recorder buffered its CSV and flushed on close, so the file was behind by up to a buffer's worth of
rows at any instant.  sim/truth_rx.py now opens it line-buffered and flushes its header
().  That it is NECESSARY, on the real process (_stage/s15-flush-burst.py starts the
recorder, sends 400 tap lines, and reads the file WITHOUT stopping it):
   PRE-FIX writer  (git show HEAD:sim/truth_rx.py) : rows on disk while running:   0 of 400   FAIL
   FIXED writer    (working tree)                  : rows on disk while running: 400 of 400   PASS

AND NOT SUFFICIENT, which is why the control flights exist.  RECT12-flush-control was flown with the
flush in place, and its metrics.txt still quoted a short tap:
   RECT12 metrics.txt : truth samples 62519 over 52.1 s; delivered-odometry samples 8628 over 43.1 s
   RECT12 truth_report: fanned out 10810 lines to port 14561 (of 64864 seen)
Flushing changes WHEN a row reaches the disk; it does not make the recorder STOP, and the recorder is
what the harness was racing.  The ordering is the fix.

================================================================================
== 4. A SECOND DEFECT, FOUND WHILE PROVING THE ORDERING: the recorder could not be stopped
================================================================================
run-mission.sh sends SIGTERM and waits.  The recorder's loop tested its stop flag only AFTER A SUCCESSFUL
RECEIVE, and a socket timeout took a 'continue' that skipped the test -- so once SITL went quiet (the
normal end of a sortie) the process could never leave, the wait expired, and it was SIGKILLed.  RECT15
printed its own line:
   WARNING: the truth recorder did not stop within 10 s and was killed -- tap.csv may be short
and truth_report.txt was never written for that flight.  The data was safe (the per-row flush had already
put every sample on disk) but the run's own GATE report was lost.

sim/truth_rx.py now also tests the flag in the timeout branch.  _stage/s24-stop-test.py drives the real
process -- samples, then silence, then SIGTERM -- and times the exit:
   PRE-FIX recorder (git show HEAD:sim/truth_rx.py) : STILL RUNNING 15 s after SIGTERM; killed          FAIL
   FIXED recorder   (working tree)                  : exited 0.22 s after SIGTERM, report written      PASS
These two are ONE commit because the ordering fix is unsound without the stop fix: waiting for a process
that cannot stop is exactly what produced the WARNING above.

== 7. THE TWO DEFECTS ARE INDEPENDENT -- which fix addresses which symptom
================================================================================
They were found in this order, and the first one's fix is what EXPOSED the second.  A reader who wants
to know whether a symptom is still possible needs them apart:

DEFECT (a) -- THE WRAP-UP ORDERING in tools/run-mission.sh.
   SYMPTOM: metrics.txt quotes a tap shorter than the flight's own truth_report.txt, and its A4 block is
            computed from that shortened tap.  The acceptance rows are unaffected.
   CAUSE:   metrics ran while sim/truth_rx.py was still in its receive loop and SITL was still writing
            the FCU's BIN; the EXIT trap stopped them only after metrics had finished.
   PROOF IT IS THE ORDERING AND NOT THE BUFFER: RECT12 was flown with the per-row flush already in
            place and still truncated (62519 vs 64864), and the RECT13 watcher caught metrics.txt being
            written 1.1 s before the tap stopped growing.
   FIX:     tools/run-mission.sh::stop_stack -- SIGTERM the FSM/planner/LIO/sensor, SIGTERM SITL and
            WAIT for it, SIGTERM the recorder and WAIT for it, and only then run metrics.  After that
            tap.csv, lio.csv and the BIN are frozen before the reader opens them.
   STILL POSSIBLE IF: a future writer is added to the run and is not in stop_stack's list -- the list is
            the contract.

DEFECT (b) -- THE RECORDER COULD NOT BE STOPPED while the tap was quiet.
   SYMPTOM: 'WARNING: the truth recorder did not stop within 10 s and was killed', and NO
            truth_report.txt for that flight.  The TAP is complete (the per-row flush had already put
            every sample on disk); what is lost is the run's own GATE report.
   CAUSE:   sim/truth_rx.py tested its stop flag only AFTER A SUCCESSFUL RECEIVE.  A socket timeout took
            a 'continue' that skipped the test, and a silent tap is exactly what the end of a sortie
            looks like once SITL stops -- so the loop could not leave and had to be SIGKILLed.
   PROOF:   RECT15 printed the warning and has no truth_report.txt; _stage/s24-stop-test.py on the real
            process: PRE-FIX 'STILL RUNNING 15 s after SIGTERM', FIXED 'exited 0.22 s after SIGTERM,
            report written'.
   FIX:     sim/truth_rx.py tests the flag in the socket-timeout branch as well.
   THE PER-ROW FLUSH () IS A SEPARATE, NECESSARY PIECE: it is what makes the tap complete
            up to the instant the recorder dies, and it is what the burst test measures (PRE-FIX 0 of
            400 rows on disk while running, FIXED 400 of 400).  It is not the ordering fix and the
            ordering fix is not it -- RECT12 is the flight that proves the difference.
   STILL POSSIBLE IF: the recorder grows another blocking call that does not check the flag.  The
            contract is 'the stop flag is tested on every pass of the loop', and the harness's WARNING
            line is the detector.
