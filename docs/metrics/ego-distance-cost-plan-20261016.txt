=== QUESTION 1, MADE EXACT: WIRING THE DISTANCE COST TO THE LAUNCH PARAMETERS ======================
The first flight of the term used a LOCAL CONSTANT (`lambda_collision = 0.5`) because the build refused
`node->get_parameter(...)` with `'node' was not declared in this scope` -- and the reason is now known, read from
the header: **the node handle is a PARAMETER of the initialiser, not a member**:

    bspline_optimizer.h:95     void setParam(rclcpp::Node::SharedPtr node);

and `dist0_` -- which the same style of call sets (`node->get_parameter("optimization/dist0", dist0_);`, seen at
`bspline_optimizer.cpp:28`, inside `setParam`) -- IS a member, which is why `cps_.clearance = dist0_` compiles
inside the class and the parameter read does not.  So the wiring is three lines, and they are known:

  1. in the class (bspline_optimizer.h, beside the other members):
         rclcpp::Node::SharedPtr node_;          // the handle setParam is given, kept so cost terms can read
  2. in setParam, where the other parameters are read:
         node_ = node;
  3. in `calcFieldDistanceCost`, replacing the constant:
         double lambda_collision = 0.5;
         node_->get_parameter("optimization/lambda_collision", lambda_collision);

WHY IT MATTERS RATHER THAN BEING COSMETIC: with the constant, the launch file's `optimization/lambda_collision`
and `optimization/dist0` STILL multiply nothing, so the flown term was "the shape with 0.5 / 0.5" and the two
parameters that were supposed to drive it have never actually been used in this planner.  Wiring them makes the
term tunable from the launch file like every other weight, and -- more importantly for this investigation --
makes `dist0` mean what upstream's name says for the first time.

AND THE OTHER TWO QUESTIONS, unchanged and still open: whether the `rc=1` of the term's one flight was the term
destabilising the optimiser (a term that does that would show exactly this way, and it is the first thing to look
at), and whether the 0.20 m footprint is the right bar for this planner on this mission.

=== ROUND 72: THE FLIGHT IS CROOKED BECAUSE THE REFERENCE IS A STAIRCASE -- AND A CORRECTION ==============
The owner looked at the top view and said: "飞的歪歪扭扭,我感觉是没跟踪上规划轨迹".  They are right in substance, and
measuring it properly also corrects a claim THIS FILE made in round 71.

CORRECTION FIRST.  Round 71 wrote "the aircraft-to-plan distance is median 0.010 m, 95th 0.172 m -- ego flew its
own plan".  That was a **nearest-neighbour** measure: the distance from each aircraft sample to the CLOSEST point
anywhere in the 560349-point plan stream, which flatters a lagging aircraft because the path it is chasing passes
near where it already is.  The honest measure uses `setpoints.csv`, the one file where the COMMAND and the FCU's
OWN POSITION sit in the same row with the same timestamp, so no clock juggling is needed:

    the same-instant command-vs-aircraft error, over the 77.0 s of wire traffic
      median 0.015 m | 75th 0.173 m | 90th 0.749 m | 95th 1.295 m | max 2.254 m
      share of the flight with the error above 0.50 m: **13.63%**
      share above 1.00 m:                            **7.69%**
    the best-fit lag: **0 ms** (the smallest median error is at zero delay), so this is NOT a constant lag
    vertical: median 0.098 m, 95th 0.746 m, max 1.258 m

So the aircraft sits on its commanded point to 1.5 cm most of the time and is **more than a metre away from it for
about 8% of the flight**.  Both statements are true; the second is the one that describes the picture.

WHY: THE REFERENCE IS A STAIRCASE, NOT A TRAJECTORY.
The /plan/cmd stream itself -- 10133 messages at 100.0 Hz over 101.3 s (`traj.csv.cmd.csv`) -- is measured:

    identical to the previous sample (a HELD value):   4584 of 10132 = **45.2%**
    hold runs: 637; median 0.05 s, 95th 0.07 s, and the longest are **1.99, 1.99, 1.99, 1.68, 1.68 s**
    when it does move: up to 0.020 m per 10 ms message (2 m/s), and the largest single-message steps are
      **-1.788 m in z** (1.867 -> 0.080 m at t = 100.42 s) and **3.406 m horizontally** (t = 17.90 s)
    only **2 distinct trajectory_id** in the whole stream (id 1 for 45.8 s, id 2 for 55.6 s): the id changes only
      at a waypoint, so the stream is NOT a rapid succession of new trajectories -- it is one long-lived reference
      that freezes and jumps.

The longest holds are **1.99 s, i.e. exactly the adapter's `stale_s = 2.0 s`**, and the adapter's own code says
what that means (`src/planner/ego_source.py:206-232`):

    if age <= self.stale_s:   t = now - start_wall;  p, v, _a = self.spline.at(t)      # sample ego's spline
    else:                     p = self.target if self.p is None else self.p;  self.held += 1   # HOLD

A spline older than two seconds is not sampled; the last commanded point is held instead (and the stream keeps
talking, which is what the supervisor's silence gate wants).  The counters that would say WHICH branch fired --
`spline(s) received`, `samples published`, `held` -- are printed by `summary()` **only at exit**, and `traj.log`
has no such line: the run's own record cannot answer it.  **That is the instrument to add** (print the counters
periodically, and log the sampled `t`, the spline's age and its duration once a second).

TWO FURTHER FACTS THAT MATTER FOR THE FIX:
  * **there is no velocity or acceleration feed-forward.**  Every /plan/cmd message carries v = 0 and a = 0 (100.0%
    of messages; the wire's vel_x/vel_y/vel_z are likewise all zero).  This is by design -- `ego_source.py:7`
    states "position = the point being flown to; velocity/acceleration are ignored on the wire" -- so the FCU flies
    a pure position loop with no feed-forward term, and the vertical error alone (median 0.098 m, 95th 0.746 m)
    is what a position-only reference produces;
  * **the reference's own average speed is 0.58 m/s** (59.0 m of stream travel over 101.3 s) while the route is
    ~10 m long: the stream travels the route roughly six times, which is the same count as the five climb-dive
    cycles in z measured in round 71.  Two independent measurements, one period.

SO: the crooked flight is not a controller-bandwidth problem and not a constant lag; it is a reference that holds
still 45% of the time, jumps up to 3.4 m in a single message, and is chased by an aircraft with no feed-forward.
The next step is instrumentation, not gains: make the adapter say what it is doing (`held`/`received` counters and
the sampled `t` against the spline's age and duration), then re-fly once and read it.

=== ROUND 73: THE REFERENCE FIX -- S1 TELEMETRY, S2 THE FAULT, S3 THE LOOKAHEAD (and a first version the

=== ROUND 79: THE CORRECTION -- "THE FCU'S OWN POSE" WAS THE SIMULATOR'S TRUTH ==============================

THE OWNER SAID: "他拿的是 31、32 转过去的信息" -- and he was right that the source was a converted stream, and
righter than he knew about WHICH one.  R1 (round 76) fed ego `/sim/truth/odom` and every record since called that
"the FCU's own estimate".  **It is not.**  That topic is republished by `sim/truth_rx.py` from the SITL **truth
tap**, and the tap says what it is in its own comment
(`ardupilot/libraries/SITL/SIM_Aircraft.cpp:708-716`):

    "Truth tap: one ASCII line per model step with **the simulator's own state**, for external estimators (the
     LIO) that need a ground truth far finer than SIMSTATE.  Frames are SITL-native: position/velocity NED
     (origin = the simulator origin, i.e. the EKF origin) ..."

So what ego planned on was **SIMULATED GROUND TRUTH**: a channel that never passes through the EKF, that exists
only in SITL, and that cannot be fed to a planner on a real aircraft at all.  The rationale written into round 76
-- "the EKF's innovation gates stand between the sensor and the planner" -- was therefore **false**, and it is
corrected here rather than left standing.  (For the record: the FCU's own estimate is MAVLink
`LOCAL_POSITION_NED` **#32** plus `ATTITUDE`/`ATTITUDE_QUATERNION` **#30/#31**; the bridge has always read #32
for its own state, `bridge/mavlink_impl.py:93,115-119`, and the supervisor reads it as its truth.)

WHAT WAS CHANGED, so that ego reads WHAT THE FLIGHT CONTROLLER BELIEVES:
  1. **the bridge now publishes the FCU's estimate** (`bridge_node.py --fcu-odom-topic`, W frame, ENU, z up, pose
     AND velocity, taken from the same `LocalState` the FSM uses) -- one flag, off by default so no earlier
     sortie's behaviour changes;
  2. **`planner/fcu_pose_feed.py` consumes it** (`--in /fcu/odom`, default) and converts W -> L as before; its
     header now states exactly which stream it is, and names `/sim/truth/odom` for what it is: a TEST channel;
  3. `run-mission.sh` passes `--fcu-odom-topic /fcu/odom` only on the Route B contract, and the feed's argv is
     `/fcu/odom -> /ego/odom_fcu`.

TWO BRIDGE DEFECTS THE BENCH FOUND ON THE WAY, both worth their own line:

  * **THE REQUEST MUST BE REPEATED.**  `MAV_CMD_SET_MESSAGE_INTERVAL` sent before the FCU's EKF has an origin is
    dropped -- `mavlink_impl.py:86-92` recorded the measurement and calls the request "a METHOD the consumer must
    verify and repeat".  Measured here: on a bare parked rig the bridge received **no #32 at all** (`st.t_us`
    stayed 0, `/fcu/odom` published nothing, ego printed "no odom." for a whole run with its subscription
    correctly wired).  `tick()` now re-asks every 2 s while no position has arrived, up to 30 times.
  * **PUBLISH ON CHANGE, NOT ON EVERY TICK.**  The first version published the latest state on every tick and the
