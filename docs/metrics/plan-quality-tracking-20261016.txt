PLAN QUALITY AND TRACKING, PER PLAN, WITH THE ACCELERATION RESOLVED IN TAU  (2026-10-16, no flight)

Instrument: `_stage/plan-quality.py`.  Everything from ONE file -- `setpoints.csv`, whose every row carries the
command AND the FCU's own position/velocity AT THE SAME INSTANT -- so no cross-clock alignment is needed; the
plan boundaries come from `traj_server.log`'s own `NEW-SPLINE` lines (traj_id, duration, start_time).  One clock
fact had to be handled first: that file's `t_wall` is written from `time.monotonic()` while `traj_server`'s log
stamps are `time.time()`, offset 1792054448.432 (fitted by the planner audit, rms 0.78 mm/sample).  The check
that it is right: **1664 of 1664 rows fall inside the logged plan windows**.

1. WHY THE ACCELERATION IS JITTERY: IT IS A BURST AT THE HEAD OF EVERY REPLAN, NOT A PROFILE

   The plan's own acceleration demand, binned by tau = time since that plan started:

       tau band (s)      rows    median     p90     p99      max
       0.0 - 0.1          100      1.66    4.31    6.98     7.08
       0.1 - 0.3          197      3.38    5.03    6.68     6.88
       0.3 - 0.6          281      1.90    4.37    7.48    13.84
       0.6 - 1.2          436      0.00    2.78    3.70     3.91
       1.2 - 2.0          205      0.00    2.68    3.67     3.70
       > 2.0              445      0.00    0.00    0.00     0.00

   * **24.4 % of all 1664 sent setpoints demand more than the flight controller can shape (2.463 m/s2 measured;
     `WPNAV_ACCELERATION_MS` 2.5), 3.2 % demand more than 5 m/s2, and the maximum is 13.84 m/s2** -- and those
     demands live almost entirely in the first 0.6 s of each plan.
   * The `tau > 2 s` band is EXACTLY zero in median, p90, p99 and max, because those rows are plans that have
     already expired: `traj_server` holds the spline's endpoint with v = a = 0 (`traj_server.cpp:214-225`).
   * So the sequence the FCU receives is: ~0 (holding an expired plan) -> 3-7 m/s2 (a fresh plan's head) -> ~0
     again, **once per second**, with a step at each boundary rather than a continuous profile.  That IS the
     jitter.  It is generated where the reference is SAMPLED (a fresh spline is evaluated at t = 0 next to the
     previous one's tail), not by the aircraft and not by the vehicle's dynamics.

     the flown maximum 2.24 m/s.

4. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE FIXES (and it corrects the one the owner questioned)

   My earlier item 3 -- "clamp the acceleration we send to the shaper's ceiling" -- treats the symptom in the
   wrong place: the jitter is generated UPSTREAM (a fresh spline every second, whose first 0.6 s asks for
   3-14 m/s2), so clipping it downstream would hide the demand without removing the discontinuity, and DIAG33's
   abort (posvelacc, 22 m/s2 alternating) was itself a symptom of the same upstream jitter.  The order that the
   measurements support is:

     a. **Make the reference continuous where it is generated** -- re-anchor the sampled point onto the vehicle,
        keep it monotone, look ~0.5 s ahead (review step 5).  This is what removes the once-per-second
        acceleration step, and it is also what removes the 0.281 m median / 0.927 m max reference steps.
     b. **Make the plan feasible for the vehicle**: at 1.0-1.5 m/s the tracking error is 0.185 m and at
        0.5-1.0 m/s it is 0.047 m, while the plan's own margin on the tight stretch is 0.05-0.17 m.  So a plan
        whose speed stays near **~1.0 m/s** (and whose acceleration demand stays under the 2.5 m/s2 the FCU will
        shape) would be tracked inside the margin this route gives.  `max_vel` 2.0 / `max_acc` 3.0 are SOFT
        quadratic penalties and the optimiser is under no obligation to respect them -- that is a planner change,
        not a bridge change.
     c. Only after (a) and (b): reconsider the acceleration feed-forward (`posvelacc`).  Sending acceleration
        that the FCU discards is a real waste, but it is not the jitter's cause.

   The honest summary for the owner's question: **the plan is not "bad" (collision-free, min +0.048 m) but it is
   UNFEASIBLE -- too fast and too abrupt at every replan boundary -- and the aircraft, which can hold roughly
   1.0-1.4 m/s with 0.05-0.35 m of error on this route, is being asked for 1.5-2.35 m/s with acceleration bursts
   5x what its controller will shape.**
