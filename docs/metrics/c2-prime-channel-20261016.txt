C2-PRIME: JUDGING THE EGO ARM BY THE CHANNEL ITS OWN MAP LEAVES  (2026-10-16, no flight)

WHAT THIS FILE IS
  The owner's call of 2026-10-16: "0.8 m is too hard to satisfy -- is there a way to make the narrowest
  passage about 0.6 m?"  The answer adopted is a RESTATEMENT of the acceptance row, not a change to the
  world, the planner or the map.  This file records the arithmetic that makes 0.6 m the honest number, the
  distribution measured over every crossing that exists, and exactly what the restatement does and does not
  claim.  It is computed offline from recordings the runs already left behind; **no sortie was flown for it**.

THE ARITHMETIC (all of it from files in this repository, none of it estimated)
  * The field `worlds/diagonal.yaml` holds 46 pillars, r = 0.30 m.  The tightest 68 pairs sit 1.414 m apart
    between centres (a sqrt(2) lattice), i.e. **0.814 m face to face** -- measured from the yaml by the tool,
    not quoted from memory.
  * The planner never sees 0.814 m.  It sees a boolean inflated grid: `grid_map/resolution` 0.1 m,
    `grid_map/obstacles_inflation` 0.099 m, so `inf_step = ceil(0.099/0.1) = 1`
    (`plan_env/src/grid_map.cpp:653`) and `cloudCallback` hard-codes `inf_step_z = 1` (`:841`).  The
    inflation is therefore a **27-cell CUBE** around every marked voxel, not a sphere: +0.10 m on the axes
    but +0.1*sqrt(2) = **+0.141 m at the in-plane corner**.
  * So the free channel the planner works with at the tightest pair is

        0.814 - 2*(0.099) = 0.616 m   (5-6 cells of 0.10 m)  -- an axis-aligned corridor
        0.814 - 2*(0.141) = 0.532 m                            -- a 45 deg corridor, the cube's corner

    and the owner's "0.6 m" is not a relaxation of the task: it is the width the task already has in the
    only frame the planner uses.
  * Consequence: a standoff of **0.10 m from a pillar surface IS one map cell IS the edge of the channel**.
    "Stay in the channel your own map declares free" and "keep >= 0.10 m" are the same sentence, which is
    why the restated bar is 0.10 m and not an arbitrary smaller number.

WHY A DISTRIBUTION AND NOT THE MINIMUM
  The old row judged the MINIMUM over the whole run.  A crossing is 90 000-150 000 truth samples and the
  minimum is one instant of it, so the row turned on a single worst moment and on nothing else -- it cannot
  distinguish "grazed one pillar once" from "flew along the pillars for a minute".  This file therefore
  reports min, p1, p5, median and the FRACTIONS below 0.00 / 0.10 / 0.20 m, and scores four candidate bars,
  so that whichever bar is adopted is adopted with its own pass count on the record.

INSTRUMENT
  `_stage/c2-prime.py` -- truth from `tap.csv` (SITL truth tap, mapped exactly as `src/tools/metrics.py`
  maps it for C2), plan from `traj.csv.points.csv` (the per-plan SUMMARY `traj.csv` carries `clr_m`, one
  number per committed plan; the points are beside it), pillars from the world file's analytic cylinders with
  their z0/z1.  Restricted to the DIAG series, i.e. to sorties actually flown on THIS field -- measuring an
  older sortie from another world against this pillar list would report numbers about nothing.

WHAT IS ADOPTED (owner's decision, 2026-10-16) -- one row becomes two

    arm holds this by a wide margin (0.562 m with 0 of 56 696 samples inside, `PLAN.md` line 172) and

  * **C2-ego, new, for the ego arm**: (a) **95 % or more of truth samples at >= 0.10 m** from every pillar

WHAT THIS DOES **NOT** CLAIM
  1. It is a RELAXATION and is recorded as one: 0.10 m is half of 0.20 m, and it was chosen because
     0 of 32 existing crossings meet 0.20 m, not because 0.20 m was shown to be geometrically impossible.
     In a 0.814 m gap a 0.20 m standoff leaves a 0.414 m band for the vehicle's centre while the channel is
     0.616 m wide, so the old row was satisfiable in principle; what it is not satisfiable by is THIS planner
     on THIS field (0 of 32, mean -0.101 m, sd 0.143 m), for the two reasons rows 73-88 established: the
     search returns the shortest path through a corridor because every A* step costs a constant
     (`dyn_a_star.cpp:217`) and this swarm variant has no obstacle-distance cost at all
     (`calcDistanceCostRebound` measures against other drones' points -- empty for one vehicle), and the map
     is a single-scan shell rebuilt per message, so a pillar closer than ~0.50 m is often not marked at all.
  2. It does NOT say the current ego arm passes.  The newest crossing, `DIAG34-routeb-pv`, FAILS the restated
     bar too: 3.20 % of samples inside a pillar body and 6.63 % below 0.10 m.  The restatement moves the
     question from an unreachable row to a discriminating one -- it does not answer it.
  3. It deliberately does NOT use the map-frame form of the same sentence ("the truth never enters a cell
     ego's own map marks OCCUPIED") as the bar.  That form is measurable from the DIAG/2 slices and is
     reported as a DIAGNOSTIC, but it can be satisfied by blindness -- an unmarked pillar is a free cell --
     and a bar that a blind map can pass is exactly the kind of instrument this investigation has had to
     correct three times.
  4. The pass counts below are the HISTORICAL record, not a fresh A/B: the runs differ in configuration
     (relay window 0/3/8/15 s, `dist0` 0.5/0.8, inflation 1/2/3 cells, four different samplers), so they must
     not be read as a comparison of those configurations against each other.  What they are is the field's
     measured distribution under the new row.
