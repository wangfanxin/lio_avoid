THE MAP'S INFLATION: WHAT IT IS, WHAT IT IS NOT, AND WHERE THE EXTRA THICKNESS COMES FROM  (2026-10-16)

THE OWNER'S QUESTION, AND IT CORRECTED ME: "why is ego taking a VOXEL?  shouldn't it take the raw per-frame
cloud and do its own processing?"

1. THE OWNER IS RIGHT, AND `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` WAS WRONG

   That file said `/lio/cloud_world` is the LIO's VOXEL map, and I repeated it.  The source says otherwise:

     livox_360.yaml:84   lio.output.map: true
     ROSWrapper.cpp:168  node.declare_parameter<bool>("lio.output.map", false); get_parameter(..., g_visual_map)
     super_lio.cpp:570   if (g_visual_map) { pcl::transformPointCloud(*scan_undistort_full_, *world_pc, T);
                                              pub_cloud_world(world_pc, ...); }
                    :582  else            { pcl::transformPointCloud(*ds_undistort_, *world_pc, T); ... }

   So the published cloud is **`scan_undistort_full_`** -- the CURRENT FRAME, undistorted and transformed into the
   world frame.  The 0.2 m `voxel_fliter_size` applies to `ds_undistort_`, i.e. to the MATCHER's copy (the `else`
   branch), and `ds_size` 0.15 to the internal map; **ego never sees either**.  `ARCHITECTURE.md` now carries the
   correction with the line numbers.

2. SO THE EXTRA THICKNESS IS OURS, AND IT ACCOUNTS FOR THE MEASUREMENT EXACTLY

   Measured, from the run's own DIAG/2 occupancy slices (the array A* and the collision check read; 59 slices, 32
   at flight height, 752 pillar-slice observations, `_stage/map-inflation.py`):

       the pillar's true surface ......................... 0.300 m from the axis
       the parameter implies the mark should reach ....... ~0.399 m (surface + one cell)
       the FIRST marked cell on a ray, median ............ 0.120 m   <- free space reaches 0.180 m INSIDE
       the LAST  marked cell on a ray, median ............ 0.540 m
       => the map's obstacle is 0.42 m thick on a 0.30 m pillar, i.e. 0.240 m PAST the surface

   And the pipeline predicts the OUTER edge with no cloud smear at all (`_stage/inflation-arithmetic.py`):

       a surface point at r = 0.300 m marks the 0.10 m cell containing it .. outer face up to 0.400 m
       + the 27-cell cube inflation, axis .................................. 0.499 m
       + the same, diagonal (the worst case a plan meets) .................. **0.540 m**
       MEASURED median last mark ........................................... **0.540 m**

   **Predicted and measured agree to the millimetre, so no LIO-side change is needed to explain the outer
   thickness** -- and the lever I proposed last round (`lio.sensor.voxel_fliter_size` 0.2 -> 0.1) is the WRONG
   LEVER: it does not touch the published cloud at all.

3. WHAT THE REAL LEVERS BUY, IN CORRIDOR TERMS (the tightest pair is 1.414 m between axes)

       configuration                              outer mark    free corridor between two pillars
       now: cell 0.10, inflation 0.099 ......... 0.540 m        0.334 m
       inflation -> 0.0 ........................ 0.400 m        0.614 m   <- nearly doubles it, one launch line
       resolution 0.05 + inflation 0.049 ....... 0.419 m        0.575 m   <- 8x memory + the A* pool edit

   So `grid_map/obstacles_inflation` 0.099 -> 0.0 is the cheap, large lever, and it is also arguably the more
   CORRECT one: the marks are already a full cell fat from cell quantisation alone, so the extra cube is a
   second helping of margin that the map cannot distinguish from the obstacle itself.

4. AND THE OPPOSITE DEFECT, WHICH NO INFLATION CHANGE TOUCHES

   Free space reaches 0.120 m from the axis, i.e. **0.180 m INSIDE** the pillar.  Cell + cube cannot produce
   that (the cell containing a surface point has its inner face at >= 0.20 m).  This is the hollow-annulus defect
   the perception audit left UNRESOLVED, and it is the dangerous one: it makes the map **optimistic** exactly
   where a plan can cut a corner into the pillar's own volume.  The measurement that would settle it: record
   `/ego/cloud` at 5 Hz during a flight -- if the cloud itself carries points inside the pillar the fault is
   upstream of our relay (lidar grazing incidence or the LIO), and if it does not, the fault is in how the map
   turns points into cells.

5. WHAT THIS DOES **NOT** CHANGE

   The collision of 2026-10-16 was not caused by the inflation: the plan's own margin (5-17 cm) and the tracking
   error (0.4-0.9 m) differ by an order of magnitude, so margin work is necessary but not sufficient.  This
   measurement matters because it says WHICH margin lever is real -- and because it removes a wrong one from the
   list before a flight is spent on it.
