COLLISION FORENSICS: FLIGHT A -- THE PLAN WAS CLEAN AND THE AIRCRAFT LEFT IT  (2026-10-16, no flight)

THE QUESTION.  The aircraft still enters a pillar.  The first thing to establish is WHICH HALF of the stack is
responsible, because "the planner drew a line through a pillar" and "the aircraft did not fly the line it was
given" look identical from the outside and have nothing in common as fixes.  This file answers that with the
run's own recordings; the instruments are named so every number can be recomputed.

1. THE PLAN WAS CLEAN, AND THE AIRCRAFT WAS NOT  (`_stage/flightA-plan-vs-truth.py`, `plan-frame-check.py`)

   Flight A (`logs/missionA-FLIGHTA-routeb-hold`):

   **So the two runs fail for DIFFERENT reasons, and only the second one implicates the planner.**  In Flight A

2. HOW BIG THE EXCURSION IS  (`_stage/tap-timebase.py`, `flightA-when.py`)

   * the tap runs at **1200.5 Hz** (median dt 0.000833 s, monotone, no duplicate stamps, one instance) -- NOT the
     93 Hz of the republished `/sim/truth/odom` topic, so 397 samples is a **0.33 s** episode, not four seconds;
   * the worst sample is at W(-2.57, -4.35) at **1.73 m** altitude, 0.166 m from the centre of the pillar at
     (-2.50, -4.50) -- i.e. 0.136 m inside its 0.30 m surface;
   * ground samples (altitude <= 0.30 m) never come closer than **+0.200 m**, so nothing is entered while
     landing, and the post-COMPLETED window is clean.

3. WHY IT WAS ENOUGH TO CLIP: THE MARGIN IS SMALLER THAN THE ERROR  (`_stage/jump-where.py`)

   * the plan's own margin at that stretch of the route is **+0.048 m** -- the trajectory runs along the bottom
     pillar row (pillars at N = -4.5, surfaces at N = -4.2) with a few centimetres to spare, which is what an
     A* whose every step costs a constant returns: the SHORTEST path through the corridor, i.e. one that hugs
     its boundary, with nothing downstream preferring the middle;
   * the COMMAND's own steps: of 4 196 pre-COMPLETED messages, 24 move by more than 0.10 m, and the largest are
     **0.81-0.99 m** -- against a p99 of 0.023 m, so the reference is smooth with a handful of metre-scale
     steps (the age rule at a replan boundary: a new spline arrives with a new `start_time` and the sampled
     point jumps).  One of those steps is **0.90 m at W(-3.86, -4.37), 1.29 m from the excursion point**, i.e.
     on the same bottom-row stretch;
   * the post-COMPLETED hold, for contrast, is exact: the step into it 0.0896 m, then **0.000000 m** over 641
     messages.

   **The mechanism that fits every number**: the plan hugs the pillars at 4.8 cm, the reference takes a
   ~0.9 m step at a replan boundary, and the aircraft -- which is a real controller with real lag -- swings
   ~1.8 m off the leg for a third of a second.  A margin of 4.8 cm cannot absorb a 0.9 m reference step.

4. A FRAME QUESTION I HAD TO SETTLE BEFORE ANY OF THIS COUNTED  (`_stage/plan-frame-check.py`)

   `traj.csv.points.csv` carries a `frame` column that reads `W`, but that string is the message's own
   `header.frame_id`, which `traj_server` hard-codes to `"world"` -- and `world` is this stack's alias for **L**
   (`docs/FRAMES.md`).  A `W` in that column is therefore NOT evidence of the W frame, and comparing the plan
   against W pillars while it is really in L would have produced plausible numbers anyway, because the pillar
   lattice is symmetric under N -> -N.  Two checks settle it, and both agree:
     * the recorder CONVERTS: `src/tools/record-traj.py` loads the same `common.frames.Calib` and writes
       `e,n,u` plus `z_min_w`/`z_max_w` (`:151`, `:131-133`), so its output is W;
     * the tour's own corners decide it independently: the file's last point reads (-1, -1) = W2 in **W**,
       while the L->W conversion of the same numbers would put it at (+1, -1), which is not this mission's
       corner (in L the corner is (-1, +1));
     * and the arithmetic agrees across two files that were measured independently: DIAG34's worst PLAN point
       reads (E +0.49, N -1.51) and DIAG34's worst TRUTH sample, measured from the tap alone, reads
       (E +0.48, N -1.52).
   Under the reading that the file is W, Flight A's plan is clean (0 inside) and DIAG34's is not (2 264 inside);
   under the other reading both become implausibly dirty (5.2 % and 12.5 % of all plan points inside a body).

5. WHAT THIS DOES **NOT** ESTABLISH

   * It does not explain why the incident is 0.33 s and not longer, nor why only ONE such step sits on that

   * It does not place the DIAG34 / `setpoints.csv` discrepancy (`STATUS.md` row 147): the setpoint row nearest
